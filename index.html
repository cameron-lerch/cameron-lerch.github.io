About me

I'm a multidisciplinary robotics engineer and researcher. My background spans autonomous systems, controls, simulation, mechanical engineering, physics, and mathematics. At Yale I earned an M.S. and M.Phil. in Mechanical Engineering as an NSF Graduate Research Fellow and advanced to Ph.D. candidacy in Prof. Ian Abraham's lab. There I developed algorithms that let teams of drones explore cluttered, unknown environments safely, combining ergodic trajectory optimization with control barrier functions, and tested them on real quadrotor swarms.

Before robotics, I spent years simulating materials and quantum phase transitions on HPC clusters, first at Missouri S&T, where I earned a B.S. in Physics summa cum laude, and then in Prof. Corey O'Hern's group at Yale. More recently, I worked where software meets hardware at Oxylus Energy, building a PyQt5 control system for multi-station lab hardware and designing a ventilated enclosure for a large-scale electrolysis stack.

I like taking an idea from theory to a working machine: researching it, developing the algorithm, writing the code, designing and prototyping the parts, and testing the physical system. Teaching matters to me just as much. I've spent years tutoring physics, mentoring students, and presenting technical work. I'm currently open to full-time roles and to consulting on mechanical engineering, software engineering, and robotics engineering projects, and I'm expanding my hands-on robotics toolkit with Viam's platform and hardware. Demonstrations

Research · ICRA 2023

Safety-Critical Ergodic Exploration

Collision-free search trajectories for single drones and drone teams in cluttered environments. Discrete control barrier functions are built into ergodic trajectory optimization, so the robots cover a space thoroughly while staying provably safe. Validated in simulation and on real quadrotors.

Details Code Paper Poster
Personal project · Viam

ViamGreeter

An autonomous trade-show booth greeter built on Viam. A fixed webcam and a pre-trained person detector track passersby, and an SO-101 robotic arm turns toward each one, points, beckons, and waves. Runs on both a simulated and a real arm.

Details Simulation video Code
Personal project · Viam

Gripper Touch-Off

A Viam module that finds where a gripper's fingertips really are by touching them to the table, the way machinists set up a tool, then corrects the gripper's frame in the machine config so motion planning stops driving fingertips into the table.

Details Code
Personal project · Viam

ViamObjectReconstruction

Finds the objects on a table, drives a Viam-controlled arm with a wrist-mounted Intel RealSense depth camera around each one, and fuses the views into a smooth, watertight 3D mesh of every object.

Details Code coming soon
Research · ICRA 2024

Energy-Aware Ergodic Search

Continuous exploration for battery-powered multi-agent teams. A battery model predicts each robot's state of charge, and planning trades battery use against coverage so at least one agent is always exploring.

Details Code Paper
Research · RSS 2024

Stein Variational Ergodic Search

Uses Stein variational inference to generate a diverse set of ergodic search trajectories at once, so a planner can choose among many good exploration strategies instead of committing to a single local optimum.

Details Paper

A swarm of Bitcraze Crazyflie 2.1 drones that flies into formation to spell a "Y". I built, calibrated, and programmed it over a single weekend, right after the drones arrived, to show prospective Ph.D. students visiting the newly opened lab.

FIRST Robotics

Robotics started for me in high school with FRC Team 1986, Team Titanium, in Lee's Summit, Missouri. I spent four seasons on the team from 2010 to 2014. For the first two I was on the mechanical subteam, designing and fabricating our competition robots. For the last two I was team captain and the robot's driver. Over those four seasons our robots won eight regional championships, and in 2013 Kraken reached the Newton Division finals at the FIRST Championship. Tutoring

I'm currently tutoring students throughout New York City. Over the years, I've spent thousands of hours teaching and mentoring students from elementary school through college across a wide range of subjects. My experience includes robotics, CAD, university-level calculus, Physics I and II, and engineering dynamics. I've also taught classes and worked with students at Yale University.

I graduated summa cum laude with a B.S. in Physics and minors in mathematics, geology, and philosophy of technology. Throughout my education, I enjoyed helping classmates outside the classroom, often organizing study sessions where we worked through challenging concepts, tackled homework, and prepared for exams together.

My strongest areas are physics, robotics, mathematics, and materials science, though I'm comfortable tutoring a broad range of subjects and academic levels. Most importantly, I enjoy helping students develop a genuine intuition for what they're learning—not simply memorize formulas or work through problems, but understand the underlying concepts and why they work.

Missouri S&T Astronomical Research Society (STARS)

Co-founder & Vice President  •  September 2016 – May 2018  •  Missouri S&T

I helped found and run Missouri S&T's Astronomical Research Society, a group of astronomy-loving students from across campus. Members met once a week, taking turns presenting something related to astronomy. When the weather was right we hosted viewing events at the on-campus observatory, which drew a mix of students and families from the local community.

  • Communicated with members each week about meeting times, events, and announcements.
  • Coordinated community outreach events at Missouri S&T's observatory.
  • Managed the society's equipment, including the telescope and astronomy textbooks.
  • Kept the society's Facebook and other social media pages up to date.

Burns & McDonnell Battle of the Brains

2nd place  •  2014  •  Summit Technology Academy

Battle of the Brains is Burns & McDonnell's STEM competition in which students from across the Kansas City area propose a new exhibit for Science City at Union Station. My class at Summit Technology Academy, led by me and one of my classmates, put together our submission, "Wright into the Future," and I developed the entire CAD model of the exhibit.

Our entry took second place, standing out among thousands of entries and winning our school $5,000.
