Molecular Dynamics Simulations of Granular Materials

Simulations of bulk metallic glasses in LAMMPS, and of soft, deformable particles using MATLAB and LAMMPS.

O'Hern Group

Overview

In Prof. Corey O'Hern's group at Yale, first as a 2018 summer research fellow and then as a Ph.D. student from 2019 to 2021, I used molecular dynamics to study how amorphous and granular materials behave.

2018 Summer Undergraduate Research Fellowship

I first joined the group through Yale's SURF CRISP program (June – August 2018), a summer research program for undergraduates from other universities, while I was a physics undergraduate at Missouri S&T:

  • Developed high-performance molecular dynamics simulations of bulk metallic glasses in LAMMPS and studied their response to applied deformation.
  • Calculated particle rearrangement statistics during cyclic deformation to characterize ductility.
  • Presented "Encoding Multidirectional Memory in Sheared Amorphous Solids" at the Leadership Alliance National Symposium in Hartford, CT (July 27, 2018), and became a member of the Leadership Alliance, a national consortium that develops students into leaders in academia, the public sector, and industry.

Projects

  • Bulk metallic glasses: simulated glass-forming ability and phase transitions, and calculated particle-rearrangement statistics under cyclic deformation to characterize ductility. Presented as "Encoding Multidirectional Memory in Sheared Amorphous Solids" at the 2018 Leadership Alliance National Symposium.
  • Clogging in hopper flows: simulated soft, deformable particles in MATLAB and LAMMPS to study when and how hopper flows clog, working with experimental collaborators.
  • Infrastructure: built parallelized simulation workflows on HPC clusters for large-scale computation.
